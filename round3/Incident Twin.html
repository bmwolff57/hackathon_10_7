Incident Twin
INCIDENT TWIN · HOW IT WORKS

One live incident, not 300 separate calls

When call volume spikes, Incident Twin groups every report from 911, 311, web forms and responder radio into one live picture of each incident: where it is, how it's moving, and whether it's getting worse. Front-line staff can flag a grouping but never finalize it. A dispatch supervisor reviews every grouping across the floor and makes the final call before units go out from CAD.

01

Ingest

Every report, transcribed live while the call is still going.

  • 911 voice (baseline)
  • 311, tip line, web forms
  • Responder radio updates
02

Categorize

Link each report to an incident, using place, time, path and details.

  • Duplicate · Update
  • Consequence · Separate
03

Prioritize

Rank incidents, not calls. Feeds existing priority codes.

  • Severity trend
  • Call volume spike
  • New urgent detail
04

Visualize

Show the incident. Hide the calls until someone asks.

  • Footprints on a map
  • Alerts on change only
05

Escalate & decide

Every grouping goes up to the dispatch supervisor, who sees all incidents at once and makes the final call.

  • Approve, split or send back
  • Front line flags, never finalizes
  • Then units go out in CAD

The one ruleAI proposes, a senior person decides. Call-takers and dispatchers can flag a grouping, but only the dispatch supervisor, who sees every grouping on the floor, can approve, split or close one. No report is ever deleted, only linked. Medical, weapon and trapped-person reports are never held for grouping; they go out straight away. Every flag and decision is logged with who made it and why.
